# The Organic Growth Playbook for App Founders

**Research report for Wildfire — fully cited.** Verified 2026-06-09 with live web sources.

This is the evidence base behind Wildfire. Every method below follows the same shape:
**what it is → why it works (the mechanism) → the proof (named company + real numbers +
fetched source) → a step-by-step playbook a solo founder can run this week.**

A note on honesty, because the buyer is skeptical: a few famous growth stats circulate in
garbled forms. We checked them. Where a number only traces back to secondary marketing
blogs rather than a primary source, it is flagged **[secondary]**. Where a number is a
company's own press-unverified claim, it is flagged **[self-reported]**. We would rather
lose a punchy stat than print one we can't stand behind.

## 0. The single most important idea: "viral" is mostly engineered broadcast, not magic

Before any tactic, the honest framing. Most "overnight viral" stories are not a
self-sustaining loop where every user brings >1 new user forever. They are a **big
broadcast** — a PR hit, an influencer, a platform algorithm, a Hacker News front page —
that puts the product in front of a large audience at once, *plus* a product good enough
to keep the people who arrive.

Lenny Rachitsky lays this out using Yahoo's research on Twitter diffusion: **"more than 90
percent of the messages didn't diffuse at all,"** and **"around 95 percent of news that
people see on Twitter comes directly from its original source"** — i.e. one big broadcast,
not a chain. ([lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/virality-is-a-myth-mostly))

**Why this matters for Wildfire:** it reframes the whole job. You don't sit around hoping
for a self-replicating loop. You **manufacture repeated shots at a broadcast** — and the
single cheapest, most repeatable broadcast machine available to a solo founder in 2026 is
short-form video, because the platforms (TikTok, Reels) will hand a brand-new account a
large test audience for *every single post*, for free. That is the entire premise of
Wildfire, and it is the one part of "going viral" a founder actually controls.

## 1. Referral & viral loops (and the k-factor math)

### What it is
A built-in mechanism where using the product naturally exposes new people to it — a
storage bonus for inviting a friend, a "skip the line" referral, a footer link on every
email.

### Why it works (mechanism)
You convert your existing users into a (free) acquisition channel. The math is the
**k-factor (viral coefficient)**:

> **K = (invites sent per user) × (conversion rate per invite)**
> ([wallstreetprep.com](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/viral-coefficient/))

- **K > 1** → each user brings more than one new user → exponential growth with no paid spend.
- **K between 0.2 and 1.0** → viral is a meaningful *tailwind* on top of other channels.
- **K < 0.2** → viral is essentially noise.
([wallstreetprep.com](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/viral-coefficient/))

Reality check: sustained K > 1 is extremely rare. For B2B SaaS, a K over **0.2 is already
"quite good."** ([command.ai](https://www.command.ai/blog/viral-coefficient-saas/)) So
treat referrals as an *amplifier*, not a standalone strategy.

### The proof
- **Dropbox** added a two-sided referral (free storage for referrer *and* referee) and
  grew **100K → 4M users in ~15 months (~3900%)**, with users sending **~2.8M invites/month
  by early 2010** — no paid marketing and no full-time marketer.
  ([referralrock.com](https://www.referralrock.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program/)) **[secondary]**
- **PayPal** paid *cash* — initially **$20**, split to **$10/$10** each side — and went
  from **~1M (Mar 2000) → ~5M users (Sep 2000)**. It found referral was its **lowest CAC of
  any channel**; total program spend was est. **$60–70M**; eBay bought it for **$1.5B** in
  2002. ([growsurf.com](https://growsurf.com/blog/paypal-referral-program/)) **[secondary]**
- **Uber's** two-sided ride-credit referral ($10, later $20/$30) reportedly returned
  **~12x ROI** per referred rider.
  ([referralrock.com](https://referralrock.com/blog/uber-referral-program/)) **[secondary]**
- **Hotmail** is the cleanest loop ever shipped: a one-line footer, *"PS I Love You. Get
  your free email at Hotmail,"* drove **0 → 12M users in ~18 months** when the entire
  internet was ~70M people (so roughly **1 in 6 internet users**), adding up to **20K/day**;
  Microsoft bought it for **~$400M**.
  ([themarketingmillennials.com](https://themarketingmillennials.com/articles/2023-01-09/the-unknown-story-of-how-hotmail-grew-to-12-million-users-in-1-5-years/)) **[secondary]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Find your "natural exposure" moment.** Where does using your app already touch another
   person? (A shared file, an invite, an exported result, an outbound email.) Put your
   loop *there*, like Hotmail's footer — not buried in a settings page.
2. **Make the reward something users actually want and that costs you ~nothing** — Dropbox
   gave storage, not cash. For an app: extra credits, a premium feature, more usage.
3. **Reward both sides.** Every high-performing program above (Dropbox, PayPal, Uber) was
   two-sided.
4. **Instrument K.** Track invites-sent-per-user and invite→signup conversion. If
   K < 0.2, stop polishing the referral and put that energy into content/broadcast instead.
5. **Don't expect K > 1.** Use referrals to *stretch* every new cohort you acquire from
   other channels, not to replace them.

## 2. Waitlists, exclusivity & scarcity

### What it is
Gating access — a waitlist, invite-only beta, "skip the line" referral — to create demand
pressure and signal that the product is worth wanting.

### Why it works (mechanism)
Three forces stack: **scarcity** (limited access raises perceived value), **social proof**
(a long list signals others want in), and a **referral loop** when you let people jump the
queue by inviting friends. It also lets you control onboarding quality.

### The proof
- **Robinhood** built a waitlist where your *position* improved by referring friends —
  "skip the line." It hit **~1M signups before launch** on roughly **$0 pre-launch ad
  spend**, the copy hit **#1 on Hacker News**, and it grew to **6M+ users in ~2 years**.
  ([prefinery.com](https://www.prefinery.com/blog/referral-programs/prelaunch-campaign/robinhood/)) **[secondary]**
- **Superhuman** used its waitlist as a genuine **capacity gate**, not a gimmick. CEO Rahul
  Vohra: *"We won't onboard you until we believe the product is ready for how you use email
  and until we have specialist capacity to deliver a great onboarding experience."* The
  list stretched to **months** from 2017–2020.
  ([review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)) **[primary]**
- **Clubhouse** went invite-only (iOS only for its first year): **~3.5M downloads in early
  Feb 2021 → ~8.1M by Feb 15**, crossing **10M still invite-only**; invite codes resold for
  **~$400 on eBay**. ([Wikipedia, cited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clubhouse_(app)))
  **[secondary]**
  **⚠ Cautionary tale:** scarcity is a *fuse, not an engine*. Clubhouse opened to everyone
  in July 2021 and momentum collapsed — early adopters lost interest as the experience
  diluted. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clubhouse_(app)))

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Stand up a referral-powered waitlist** (a single email field → a "you're #X in line,
   move up by sharing" page). This is the Robinhood mechanic and it's a weekend build.
2. **Make the queue visible and gamified** — show position, show how many referrals to jump.
3. **Have a real reason for the gate** — limited onboarding slots, server capacity, a
   founder-led setup call. A fake gate gets sniffed out.
4. **Convert the list into a launch broadcast.** A waitlist's real value is everyone gets
   emailed on the same day → your manufactured broadcast moment (see §0).
5. **Plan for "after the velvet rope."** Clubhouse's lesson: have a retention/quality plan
   before you open the gates, or the spike evaporates.

## 3. Launch platforms: Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, Indie Hackers

### What it is
Posting your launch where early-adopter audiences already gather, to create a concentrated
spike of attention (a broadcast moment per §0).

### Why it works (mechanism)
These platforms aggregate exactly the people who try new tools and tell others. A top
placement = a one-day broadcast to thousands of high-intent users plus durable backlinks
and social proof.

### The proof — with honest outcomes (not hype)
- **Plausible Analytics** finished **#2 Product of the Day, 850+ upvotes, 27 reviews** —
  and got **2,560 visitors and 36 trials (1.38% conversion)** from it. Their own verdict:
  *"Product Hunt is not the holy grail... the faith of your startup should not rely on your
  Product Hunt launch."* In the same window, **Google organic out-converted PH (3.52% vs
  1.38%)**. ([plausible.io](https://plausible.io/blog/product-hunt-launch)) **[primary]**
- **Claap's** PH launch: **1,400 upvotes, ~1,000 signups launch week, ~3,000 over six
  weeks**, contributing to **~$60K revenue**.
  ([growthmentor.com](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/product-hunt-launch/)) **[secondary]**
- **Siftery's** PH launch drove **6,000+ users — more than a later TechCrunch article did.**
  ([growthmentor.com](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/product-hunt-launch/)) **[secondary]**
- **Robinhood's** launch copy hit **#1 on Hacker News**, which amplified the waitlist to the
  masses. ([prefinery.com](https://www.prefinery.com/blog/referral-programs/prelaunch-campaign/robinhood/)) **[secondary]**
- **Common success factor across #1 finishers:** the Tally team spent **6+ months in
  communities and outreach** building a warm audience *before* launch day; top launches
  ship a **launch video.** ([growthmentor.com](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/product-hunt-launch/)) **[secondary]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Pick ONE platform that matches your audience.** Devs → Hacker News / a relevant
   subreddit. Indie/SaaS → Product Hunt + Indie Hackers. Don't spray all four.
2. **Build a warm list first.** The single biggest predictor of a good launch is having
   people *ready* to show up (your waitlist from §2, an email list, an X following).
3. **Make a 30–60s launch video.** Every #1 finisher had one.
4. **For Reddit/HN, give value, don't pitch.** Lead with a genuinely useful post or a
   build story; the product is the supporting actor.
5. **Set honest expectations.** Treat a launch as *one* broadcast (hundreds–low thousands of
   visitors), not as your growth strategy. Plausible's data is the reality check.

## 4. Community-led growth

### What it is
Turning users into co-creators and advocates — ambassador programs, template/asset
galleries, a place (Discord/forum) where users help each other and produce content *for*
you.

### Why it works (mechanism)
Two compounding loops: (1) users create assets (templates, files) that pull in *new* users
via search and sharing; (2) advocates produce content and run events you could never staff
yourself. It scales distribution without scaling headcount.

### The proof
- **Notion** reached **~1M users by 2019 mostly via word of mouth.** Its **Template Gallery**
  is overwhelmingly community-built (far more use-cases than Notion could cover alone), and
  its **Ambassador** call drew **~1,000 applicants in the first weeks**, reaching **a
  community event almost every day** worldwide. The real inflection (2020–2022) came when
  TikTok/YouTube creators amplified templates like "Notion for Students."
  ([review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/how-notion-does-marketing-a-deep-dive-into-its-community-influencers-growth-playbooks/)) **[secondary]**
- **Figma** grew bottom-up: one designer adopts it → introduces it to the team → it spreads
  org-wide. On launch day they ran a **script to find top designers on Twitter** and
  engaged them directly; **Figma Community (2019)** turned users into co-creators, and
  *"the most popular community templates had more installs than some standalone design tools
  had total users."* Pricing reinforced it — **free viewers, paid editors.**
  ([review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/the-5-phases-of-figmas-community-led-growth-from-stealth-to-enterprise/)) **[secondary]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Create the first 5–10 "assets" yourself** (templates, presets, example projects) and
   make them trivially shareable. This seeds the gallery loop Notion and Figma rode.
2. **Open one home for your users** — a Discord or a subreddit — and *show up daily* at the
   start. Treat questions as content.
3. **Recruit your first advocates by name.** Figma's literal tactic: find the people with
   audiences in your niche and personally invite them in.
4. **Reward contribution with status and reach**, not just money — being featured is a
   powerful motivator (Notion's ambassadors).
5. **Make assets SEO-discoverable** (e.g. "[your category] template for X") so the community
   loop feeds the search loop (§5).

## 5. Content & SEO (including programmatic SEO)

### What it is
Earning durable organic traffic by publishing content that ranks: educational articles
("product-led content"), and **programmatic SEO** — generating thousands of templated pages
that each target a long-tail query.

### Why it works (mechanism)
Search intent compounds. One great article keeps pulling in qualified traffic for years
(unlike an ad, which dies when spend stops). Programmatic SEO scales that to thousands of
"[X] + [Y]" pages capturing demand you'd never write by hand.

### The proof
- **Zapier (programmatic SEO):** one landing page per **~5,000+ supported apps**, plus
  integration-pair pages ("connect Gmail + Slack"). Per Ahrefs' teardown, **app/integration
  pages ≈ 16% of organic traffic and the blog ≈ 67.5%**; organic grew from **~1.19M → ~5M
  monthly visits in ~3 years.** ([ahrefs.com](https://ahrefs.com/blog/zapier-seo-case-study/)) **[primary case study]**
- **Ahrefs (product-led content):** grew the blog from **15K → 250K+ monthly Google
  visits in ~4 years (+~1,136%)** by matching content to keyword demand and weaving the
  product into the lesson — reaching **~$100M ARR with ~69 people and zero salespeople.**
  ([medium.com/ahrefs-marketing](https://medium.com/ahrefs-marketing/how-we-grew-traffic-to-ahrefs-blog-by-1136-and-got-thousands-of-paying-customers-1fbd7e6b145a)) **[primary, by Ahrefs' CMO]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **List 10 "problem" queries your target user types into Google** (the pain your app
   solves). Write the single best article on the top 3. That's product-led content.
2. **Find your programmatic angle:** is there a repeatable "[A] vs [B]", "[tool] alternatives",
   or "[integration] + [integration]" pattern in your space? Build one template page,
   confirm it ranks, *then* scale it.
3. **Be the product in the content** — show the tool solving the exact problem (Ahrefs'
   move), don't just write generic listicles.
4. **Accept the timeline.** SEO is a 6–18 month compounding play. Run it *in parallel* with
   short-form video (which delivers attention this week).

## 6. Organic social virality (short-form: TikTok / Reels) — Wildfire's core lane

### What it is
Posting short videos that the platform's recommendation engine pushes to a large audience —
regardless of how many followers you have.

### Why it works (mechanism) — and this is the load-bearing fact for Wildfire
TikTok's **For You** feed does **not** rank by follower count. From TikTok's own newsroom:

> **"neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos
> are direct factors in the recommendation system."**
> ([newsroom.tiktok.com](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you))

Instead the system weights **user interactions** — and analyses converge that **watch time
and completion rate are the dominant signal** (~40–50% of weight), above likes/comments,
with **shares** the highest-value engagement.
([sproutsocial.com](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-algorithm/))

The consequence: the platform **tests essentially every video on a small seed audience**,
and promotes the ones that hold attention — so **a zero-follower founder can outrank a
million-follower account if the video is better.** That makes short-form the only
"broadcast machine" (§0) a solo founder can fire *every single day, for free.*

### The proof
- **Duolingo** turned its mascot loose with fast, "unhinged," trend-native content and rode
  **the comment section as its creative brief** ("the comment section is our social brief").
  Result: **~850M organic TikTok views, 143 videos at 1M+ views, 50K → 16M followers in ~4
  years**, with DAU climbing **4.9M (2021) → 80M+ (2025).** Brand-scale, but the *mechanics*
  (jump on trends fast, mine comments for ideas, post relentlessly) transfer to any founder.
  ([startupspells.com](https://startupspells.com/p/duolingo-tiktok-playbook-850m-views-143-viral-videos)) **[secondary]**
- **Cal AI** is often cited as a "viral organic" app — but **honestly, it isn't organic
  word-of-mouth.** TechCrunch confirms a team member was *"running influencer marketing,"*
  and trade coverage describes **~150 creators on retainer (~4 posts/mo each)** plus paid
  ads. It hit **~5M downloads in 8 months** and **~$2M revenue in one month**
  (**[self-reported]**, TechCrunch could not verify). Use Cal AI as proof of **creator
  seeding at scale (§8)**, *not* as proof of free organic virality.
  ([techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/16/photo-calorie-app-cal-ai-downloaded-over-a-million-times-was-built-by-two-teenagers/)) **[primary, with caveats]**

> **A stat we deliberately are NOT printing as fact:** several blogs claim "83% of viral
> videos come from accounts under 10k followers." We could not trace it to a primary source,
> so we treat it as **unverified**. We don't need it — TikTok's own statement above proves
> the same point (follower count isn't a ranking factor) without the shaky number.

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Optimize for the first 2 seconds and the loop.** Completion rate is the #1 signal, so a
   strong hook and a video that *resends to the start* beats production value.
2. **Engineer shares.** Make videos people send to a friend ("you need to see this") — the
   highest-value signal.
3. **Post daily and read your comments.** Duolingo's edge was *volume + speed + mining
   comments for the next video.* Each post is a fresh free broadcast attempt.
4. **Make the founder/product the show**, in your specific niche. The algorithm rewards
   relevance + retention, not polish.
5. **Don't buy followers or chase a follower count** — it literally doesn't affect reach.

## 7. Build-in-public

### What it is
Sharing your real journey — revenue, metrics, decisions, failures — openly (usually on X /
Indie Hackers), building an audience that becomes your launch channel.

### Why it works (mechanism)
Transparency earns trust and attention *before* you have a product to sell, compounding an
owned audience. When you ship, you already have a built-in broadcast (§0). It also doubles
as accountability and free user research.

### The proof
- **Pieter Levels** is the canonical honest case. He spent **~10 years building a 600K X
  following across 40+ products**; when he launched **Photo AI (Feb 2023)** it did **$5.4K
  in week one → ~$132K MRR by month 18.** His bootstrapped portfolio is **~$3.1M ARR.**
  ([softwareseni.com](https://www.softwareseni.com/building-in-public-the-10-year-distribution-strategy-behind-solo-founder-revenue/)) **[secondary]**
- **Marc Lou**: **ShipFast** has done **$500K+** total; he built **TrustMRR in ~24 hours →
  $13,883 MRR in 48 hours → ~$25K/mo by Jan 2026**; **DataFast ~$15.8K/mo.**
  ([startupseries.io](https://startupseries.io/how-indie-hacker-marc-lou-monetised-mrr-bragging/)) **[secondary]**
- **The honesty signal itself:** Levels' viral note that *"there's so many fake MRR
  screenshots on here..."* and Marc Lou's response (TrustMRR, which verifies revenue via
  read-only Stripe keys) show the indie community now *punishes* fabricated numbers and
  *rewards* verifiable transparency. ([startupseries.io](https://startupseries.io/how-indie-hacker-marc-lou-monetised-mrr-bragging/)) **[secondary]**
- **Open-startup pioneers:** **Buffer** publicly shared revenue starting around **$12K/mo**
  ([buffer.com](https://buffer.com/resources/revenue-dashboard/)); **Baremetrics** made its
  full dashboard public in **Feb 2014 at ~$3K/mo**, sparking a movement hundreds of founders
  joined. ([baremetrics.com](https://baremetrics.com/blog/open-startups)) **[primary]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Pick one platform (X or Indie Hackers) and post your real progress 3–5×/week** —
   numbers, decisions, what broke.
2. **Share specifics, not platitudes.** "MRR went $0→$340 this week, here's what worked"
   beats motivational fluff.
3. **Never fabricate.** The community fact-checks (TrustMRR exists *because* of fake
   screenshots). Honest small numbers out-perform fake big ones.
4. **Convert audience → email list** so your eventual launch is a broadcast you own.
5. **Combine with short-form (§6):** a build-in-public *video* series gets both the X
   audience and the For You page.

## 8. Creator / influencer seeding

### What it is
Getting creators (often micro-creators) to make content featuring your product — gifted
access, affiliate/commission, or paid retainers — to borrow their distribution.

### Why it works (mechanism)
A creator's audience already trusts them, and on TikTok each creator's post is its own
algorithmic broadcast attempt. Many small creators = many shots on goal across many
audiences. This is the engineered-broadcast principle (§0) bought at volume.

### The proof
- **Cal AI** is the clearest modern example: **~150 creators on retainer (~4 posts/mo
  each)** plus paid ads drove **~5M downloads in 8 months** (revenue figures
  **[self-reported]**). Reporting is explicit this was **creator/influencer-driven, not
  organic word-of-mouth.** ([techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/16/photo-calorie-app-cal-ai-downloaded-over-a-million-times-was-built-by-two-teenagers/)) **[primary, with caveats]**
- **Notion's** 2020–2022 inflection was driven heavily by **TikTok/YouTube creators**
  making template content ("Notion for Students" → millions of views).
  ([review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/how-notion-does-marketing-a-deep-dive-into-its-community-influencers-growth-playbooks/)) **[secondary]**
- **Figma** seeded launch by **personally engaging top designers on Twitter** so they'd talk
  about the tool. ([review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/the-5-phases-of-figmas-community-led-growth-from-stealth-to-enterprise/)) **[secondary]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Make a list of 20 micro-creators (5K–100K) in your exact niche** — engagement > size.
2. **Offer free lifetime access + a clear affiliate cut.** Low cost, aligned incentives.
3. **Give them a hook/angle, not a script** — let them speak in their own voice (it performs
   better and reads as authentic).
4. **Seed many, double down on the few that hit.** Cal AI's model is volume → identify
   winners → retain them.
5. **Disclose.** Sponsored content must be marked; audiences (and platforms) punish stealth
   ads.

## 9. Network effects vs. virality (precise definitions)

These get conflated constantly. They are **independent** properties — and the distinction
shapes whether your growth is *defensible*.

Per **NFX**:
- **Viral effects** = *"when you get your existing customers to get you more **new
  customers**, ideally **for free**."* → about **growth / acquisition.**
- **Network effects** = *"when every customer of your product adds incremental value to all
  the other customers... so that it becomes difficult for customers to find any alternative
  product."* → about **value / defensibility.**
([nfx.com](https://www.nfx.com/post/viral-effects-vs-network-effects))

They cross independently:
- **Viral, no network effect:** JibJab, PhotoBooth, QuizUp — *"flashy but short-lived."*
  ([nfx.com](https://www.nfx.com/post/viral-effects-vs-network-effects))
- **Network effect, no virality:** a B2B marketplace that acquires both sides via paid ads.
  ([nfx.com](https://www.nfx.com/post/viral-effects-vs-network-effects))

**Implication for an app founder:** virality (and short-form content) gets you *attention
and acquisition* — the top of the funnel. To *keep* what you acquire, you still need a
product that retains (ideally with its own network or habit loop). Wildfire is honest about
this: it's a distribution engine, not a substitute for a product people stick with.

## 10. Launch sequencing, PR & timing

### What it is
Orchestrating the *order* of growth moves so they compound, instead of firing one shot and
hoping.

### Why it works (mechanism)
Per §0, growth is a series of **broadcasts**. Sequencing means each broadcast leaves behind
an *owned* asset (an email list, an audience, backlinks) that makes the next broadcast
bigger.

### The proof
- **Robinhood** sequenced it perfectly: **waitlist + referral → HN front page → 1M
  pre-launch signups → launch broadcast to that list.** Each step fed the next.
  ([prefinery.com](https://www.prefinery.com/blog/referral-programs/prelaunch-campaign/robinhood/)) **[secondary]**
- **Top Product Hunt #1s** did **months of community warm-up before** the launch-day
  broadcast — the launch *converted* an audience they'd already built.
  ([growthmentor.com](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/product-hunt-launch/)) **[secondary]**
- **Plausible** is the counter-example: a launch *without* a durable engine behind it = a
  spike, then flatline. Their actual growth came from **ongoing content + community**, not
  the launch. ([plausible.io](https://plausible.io/blog/product-hunt-launch)) **[primary]**

### Playbook for a solo founder this week
1. **Build the owned asset first** (waitlist + email list + a small content presence).
2. **Sequence:** warm-up content → waitlist with referral → coordinated launch (PH/HN +
   email + creators on the same day) → sustained short-form to keep the broadcasts coming.
3. **Re-use every broadcast.** Turn a good launch or viral video into an email, a blog post,
   a case study — each becomes a future acquisition asset.
4. **Don't bet the company on one launch day.** It's one broadcast among many.

## 11. Myths & what does NOT work (debunked)

- **Myth: "Make something great and it'll go viral on its own."** Mostly false. Yahoo's
  Twitter data: **>90% of messages don't diffuse at all**; **~95% of news seen comes
  straight from the original source.** Growth comes from *engineered broadcasts*, not
  spontaneous chains. ([lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/virality-is-a-myth-mostly))
- **Myth: "Aim for a viral coefficient above 1."** Almost no one sustains K > 1. For B2B
  SaaS, **K > 0.2 is already good** and many products sit below 0.5 because they simply
  aren't structurally viral. Treat referrals as an amplifier, not the plan.
  ([command.ai](https://www.command.ai/blog/viral-coefficient-saas/))
- **Myth: "You need a big follower count to get views."** False on TikTok by the platform's
  own admission — **follower count is not a ranking factor.**
  ([newsroom.tiktok.com](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you))
  So buying followers is wasted money.
- **Myth: "A Product Hunt #1 will make your startup."** Even a #2-of-the-day finish
  delivered Plausible only **36 trials**; Google organic out-converted it. A launch is one
  broadcast, not a growth strategy. ([plausible.io](https://plausible.io/blog/product-hunt-launch))
- **Myth: "Exclusivity/hype is durable."** Clubhouse hit 10M on scarcity, then **deflated
  when it opened up** with no retention engine underneath. Scarcity is a fuse, not an
  engine. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clubhouse_(app)))
- **Myth: "Virality = defensibility."** No. Viral acquisition without a network effect or
  retention is *"flashy but short-lived."* ([nfx.com](https://www.nfx.com/post/viral-effects-vs-network-effects))
- **Myth (and an honesty rule): "Post a big MRR screenshot to look credible."** The indie
  community now actively *distrusts* unverified revenue claims — Pieter Levels called out
  fake screenshots; Marc Lou built TrustMRR to verify them via Stripe. Fabricated numbers
  are now a *liability.* ([startupseries.io](https://startupseries.io/how-indie-hacker-marc-lou-monetised-mrr-bragging/))

## 12. Synthesis → the Wildfire framework

Pulling every method together, one throughline emerges (full version in
[`framework.md`](./framework.md)):

> **You can't manufacture a self-sustaining viral loop. You *can* manufacture repeated shots
> at a broadcast — and then keep the people each shot brings in.** The cheapest, most
> repeatable broadcast machine a solo founder controls is short-form video, because the
> platform hands every post a fresh free test audience independent of follower count.
> Referrals, waitlists, launches, communities, SEO and creator seeding are all *amplifiers*
> that make each broadcast land bigger and leave behind an owned asset.

Wildfire operationalizes the part a founder actually controls: **profile/positioning →
ideas + scripts engineered for completion & shares → a warm-up + launch sequence →
30/60/90 compounding → the underlying mechanics taught honestly.** No fake virality
promised; a disciplined system for taking and re-taking the only free broadcast available.
