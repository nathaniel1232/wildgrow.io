# Proof & Case Studies — Verified

Every row below was checked against a fetched source (verified 2026-06-09). Where a
widely-repeated figure could only be traced to secondary marketing blogs (not a primary
source), it is marked **[secondary]**. Where a figure is a company's *self-reported,
press-unverified* claim, it is marked **[self-reported]**. Treat those two flags as
"directionally true, cite with care."

|---|---|---|---|
| **Dropbox** | Two-sided referral (free storage both sides) | 100K → 4M users in ~15 months (~3900%); ~2.8M invites/month sent by early 2010; no paid marketing, no full-time marketer | https://www.referralrock.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program/ **[secondary]** |

| **Robinhood** | Waitlist + "skip the line" referral | ~1M signups *before launch* with ~$0 pre-launch ad spend; copy hit #1 on Hacker News; grew to 6M+ users in ~2 years | https://www.prefinery.com/blog/referral-programs/prelaunch-campaign/robinhood/ **[secondary]** |
| **Hotmail** | Viral footer ("PS I Love You. | **Lenny Rachitsky / Yahoo research** | Myth-buster: virality is mostly broadcast | On Twitter, "more than 90% of messages didn't diffuse at all"; ~95% of news seen comes directly from the original source. Most "viral" growth = a big broadcast, not k>1 | https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/virality-is-a-myth-mostly **[primary essay citing Yahoo/Hit Makers]** |
| **NFX** | Definition: viral ≠ network effects | Viral = existing users bring *new* users (growth). Network effects = each user adds *value* for others (defensibility). They are independent. | https://www.nfx.com/post/viral-effects-vs-network-effects **[primary, NFX]** |
