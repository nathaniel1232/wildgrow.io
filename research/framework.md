# The Wildfire Framework

The throughline that ties every method in [`viral-growth-playbook.md`](./viral-growth-playbook.md)
together — what Wildfire actually teaches and delivers.

---

## The core thesis (one sentence)

**You cannot manufacture a self-sustaining viral loop, but you *can* manufacture repeated
shots at a broadcast — and the cheapest, most repeatable broadcast machine a solo founder
controls is short-form video, because the platform hands every post a fresh free test
audience regardless of follower count.**

This is grounded, not aspirational:
- "Viral" is mostly **engineered broadcast, not spontaneous chains** — Yahoo's Twitter data
  shows **>90% of messages don't diffuse at all** and **~95% of what people see comes
  straight from the source.** ([lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/virality-is-a-myth-mostly))
- The one broadcast a founder can fire daily, for free, is short-form — because TikTok
  states **"neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing
  videos are direct factors"** in recommendations.
  ([newsroom.tiktok.com](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you))
- Sustained **K > 1 is rare**; even good SaaS often sits at **K ~0.2.** So loops *amplify*
  broadcasts — they don't replace them.
  ([command.ai](https://www.command.ai/blog/viral-coefficient-saas/))

## The framework: FIRE

A four-part loop a founder runs every week. Each turn of the loop fires a broadcast and
banks an owned asset for the next one.

### **F — Frame** (positioning)
Decide *who* the video is for and *why this app, now*. The algorithm rewards relevance +
retention, so a sharp niche beats a broad one. Figma won by being unmissable to *designers*
first; Notion's breakout content was "Notion **for Students**," not "Notion for everyone."
→ *Wildfire: onboarding derives positioning, content pillars, and target viewer.*

### **I — Ideate** (engineer for the signal)
Produce content built for the one metric that drives reach: **watch-time / completion**
(~40–50% of TikTok's weight), with **shares** as the highest-value engagement.
([sproutsocial.com](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-algorithm/)) That means a hook
in the first 2 seconds, a tight loop, and a reason to send it to a friend — not high
production value.
→ *Wildfire: the Idea Engine outputs hook → beats → on-screen text → caption → hashtags →
sound, with hook scoring; Niche Radar shows what's already winning and why.*

### **R — Run the sequence** (warm up → launch → compound)
Fire broadcasts in an order that compounds. Robinhood's textbook run: **waitlist + referral
→ Hacker News #1 → 1M pre-launch signups → launch broadcast.**
([prefinery.com](https://www.prefinery.com/blog/referral-programs/prelaunch-campaign/robinhood/))
Top Product Hunt #1s spent **months warming an audience before** launch day.
([growthmentor.com](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/product-hunt-launch/)) Volume + speed
is the engine: Duolingo posted relentlessly to **143 videos at 1M+ views.**
([startupspells.com](https://startupspells.com/p/duolingo-tiktok-playbook-850m-views-143-viral-videos))
→ *Wildfire: the Launch Playbook (set up → warm up → launch → compound) and the 30/60/90
plan from cold account to first breakout.*

### **E — Endure** (retention + amplifiers)
A broadcast that brings users you can't keep is Clubhouse — **10M, then collapse** once
scarcity opened up with no retention underneath.
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clubhouse_(app))) Virality is acquisition;
**network effects/retention are a separate, defensive property.**
([nfx.com](https://www.nfx.com/post/viral-effects-vs-network-effects)) So each loop should
(a) keep the people it brought, and (b) leave behind an *owned asset* — an email list,
referral loop, community, or backlink — that makes the next broadcast bigger. Pieter Levels'
**$5.4K week-1 → $132K MRR** rode an audience he'd compounded over a decade.
([softwareseni.com](https://www.softwareseni.com/building-in-public-the-10-year-distribution-strategy-behind-solo-founder-revenue/))
→ *Wildfire: the loop repeats; every post sharpens positioning and hooks; the plan keeps the
owned assets compounding.*

## How every researched method maps onto FIRE

| Method | Where it lives in FIRE | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Short-form video (TikTok/Reels) | **I + R** | The primary free broadcast machine |
| Referral / viral loops (Dropbox, PayPal, Uber, Hotmail) | **E** | Amplifier — stretches each cohort (K) |
| Waitlist / scarcity (Robinhood, Superhuman, Clubhouse) | **R** | Concentrates demand into a launch broadcast |
| Launch platforms (PH, HN, Reddit, IH) | **R** | A single big broadcast + owned backlinks |
| Community-led (Notion, Figma) | **E** | Compounding asset; users create + advocate |
| Content / programmatic SEO (Zapier, Ahrefs) | **E** | Durable owned broadcast that compounds for years |
| Build-in-public (Levels, Marc Lou, Buffer) | **R + E** | Builds the owned audience you broadcast to |
| Creator/influencer seeding (Cal AI, Notion creators) | **I + R** | Buys broadcast volume across trusted audiences |
| Network effects | **E** | Defensibility — keeps what virality acquires |

## The honesty layer (non-negotiable)

Wildfire's framework is also a set of guardrails the product must embody:

1. **Promise broadcasts, not magic.** We teach a repeatable system for taking the free shot
   short-form gives you — not a guaranteed viral loop. The research says that's the honest
   shape of "going viral."
2. **Distinguish organic from paid.** Cal AI is a *creator-seeding/paid* story, not organic
   word-of-mouth — and we say so. Mislabeling it would be exactly the kind of garbled claim
   a skeptical buyer catches. ([techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/16/photo-calorie-app-cal-ai-downloaded-over-a-million-times-was-built-by-two-teenagers/))
3. **Never fabricate our own numbers.** The indie community now punishes fake MRR/testimonials
   (Levels' callout → Marc Lou's TrustMRR). Wildfire uses **real external proof + honest
   early-access framing**, never invented Wildfire metrics or testimonials.
   ([startupseries.io](https://startupseries.io/how-indie-hacker-marc-lou-monetised-mrr-bragging/))
4. **Set real expectations.** A launch is one broadcast (Plausible: 36 trials from a #2
   finish). Compounding beats one-shot hope. ([plausible.io](https://plausible.io/blog/product-hunt-launch))
